\chapter{Transcription regulation}

Multicellular higher organisms, such as human, are formed by thousands of cell types. However, the genome in every of those cells is identical. If the recipe is the same, how is it possible to have so many cells types that manage to work together and run such a complex organism? The expectation of the Human Genome project was that once we have a completed human genome, all these secrets will be revealed. Unfortunately, it turned out that the genome is much more mysterious, and we are studying its secrets until this day. Even though the sequence of the DNA is the same in every cell and a set of genes does not change, transcription regulation is what allows for the cells to differentiate. Gene expression is tightly regulated by a variety of mechanisms and each gene is expressed only in a particular cell at the particular time. Genomic DNA is constantly being approached by regulatory proteins and other small molecules. And all this must be carefully regulated to ensure appropriate cell grow, division and differentiation.
